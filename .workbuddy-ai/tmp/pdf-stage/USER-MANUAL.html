1. Introduction

NEXUS is a service marketing and billing system for an internet service provider. It manages the complete life of a customer:

Enquiry  →  Order  →  Site survey  →  Installation  →  Active connection
                                                              ↓
              Payment  ←  Bill issued  ←  Monthly billing run

The system is used by five different groups of people, and each group sees only what its role allows. A retail counter clerk cannot see the accounts ledger; a customer cannot see another customer's bill.

1.1 What NEXUS does

Area What it handles
Customer registration Walk-in orders at a retail outlet, or self-service on the public site
Service feasibility Distance and capacity checks before an installation is committed
Installation Scheduling, technician assignment, commissioning a line
Catalog Dial-up, broadband and telephone plans, with hourly, monthly, half-yearly and yearly rates
Billing Monthly bill runs, advance and security deposit accounting, 12.24% service tax, bulk discounts
Payments Cash, cheque, bank transfer and card payments recorded at the counter, with auto-numbered receipts
Inventory Equipment stock, vendors, purchase orders
Reporting Revenue by city and year, connection growth, customer lists
Customer self-service Bill history, plan upgrades, feedback, order tracking

1.2 Important: how payment works

NEXUS is a billing management system, not a payment gateway. Customers do not pay online.

Payment is taken at the counter — either at a retail outlet or at the accounts office. The customer hands over cash or a cheque, and a member of staff records it in the system. The system then:

  • generates a unique receipt number (for example RCP-202610-00001),
  • marks the bill Partially Paid or Paid,
  • makes the payment visible on the customer's own dashboard immediately.

This matches the requirements: "the details of the payments will be maintained by the employees of the retail outlets and by the accounts department."

4.2 Registering a walk-in customer

This is the retail desk's most important job. Everything is captured in one four-step wizard, and a customer account, a login and an order are all created in a single action.

  1. Open New Order.
  2. Step 1 — Service. Choose the service type (Dial-Up, Broadband or

Telephone), the plan and the billing cycle (hourly, monthly, half-yearly or yearly). Greyed-out plans are not offered for that service type.

  1. Step 2 — Customer. Name, CNIC, mobile number, email, address and city.
  2. CNIC must be unique.
  3. Mobile must be unique and written with dashes, e.g. 0300-1234567.
  4. If the customer is a company, tick Corporate and give the organisation

name — this is what qualifies the account for bulk rates.

  1. Step 3 — Installation. Address, preferred date and preferred time slot.

For a telephone or dial-up plan the system asks for the landline number.

  1. Step 4 — Account. Choose the password for the customer's new login and

accept the terms.

On submit the system shows:

  • the order ID (for example B0000000005),
  • the account ID (for example B042000000000007),
  • the quoted amount (advance plus the first period's rental).

Write these down for the customer. The account ID is what they use to sign in or to quote when calling support.

4.3 Record at the counter (advance)

The amount shown at the end of the order is what the customer pays at the counter — typically the security deposit or the advance. The system does not record this automatically; the accounts department applies it against the first bill, which is why it appears on the invoice as an advance credit.

4.4 Collecting a bill payment

This is the counter payment flow.

  1. Open Bills & Payments in the sidebar.
  2. Find the customer's bill. Use the Search box with the invoice number,

account ID or customer name, or filter by status.

  1. Click View on the bill row.
  2. Scroll to the Record a Payment section at the bottom of the invoice.

It shows the outstanding balance above it.

  1. Fill in:
  2. Amount (USD) — pre-filled with the full outstanding balance. Change it

if the customer is paying part of the bill.

  • Method — Cash, Cheque, Bank Transfer or Card.
  • Reference — the cheque number or transfer reference. **Mandatory for

cheque payments.**

  • Remarks — any note you want on the ledger.
  • Click Record Payment.

The page reloads with a green confirmation showing the receipt number. The payment now appears in the Payment History table of that invoice, and the bill's status changes to Paid or Partially Paid.

If you cannot see the payment form: the bill is either still a Draft (accounts has not issued it yet) or it has nothing left to pay. Either way the page tells you which of the two it is — look for the panel at the bottom of the invoice.

4.5 Till Date Register

/Retail/Register lists everything taken at your outlet — orders placed, cash collected, connections sold. Order numbers are links; click one to open its tracking page.

5. Technical Panel

The technical panel covers the physical work — surveying a site, installing a line and handing it over to billing.

Sign in: T0000000000001 / Nexus@2026

5.1 The installation workflow

Every new order passes through this sequence. The order cannot skip a step.

Awaiting Feasibility
        ↓  RecordCheck
Feasibility passed
        ↓  Confirm
Scheduled
        ↓  StartInstallation
Installation in progress
        ↓  CompleteInstallation
Connection created (Pending)
        ↓  ChangeStatus → Active
Active — now billable

5.2 Step by step

  1. Open Installations (/Operations). This is the survey queue — orders

waiting for a site visit.

  1. Record Check. Open the order and fill in:
  2. Distance from exchange (km) — the SRS rule is that a site more than

5 km from the exchange cannot be served.

  • Exchange has capacity — yes or no.
  • Landline verified — required for dial-up and telephone orders.
  • Remarks.

If the distance exceeds 5 km, or capacity is unavailable, the check fails and the order is rejected. This is a hard business rule.

  1. Confirm. Set the installation date and slot. The order moves to

Scheduled.

  1. Start Installation. Press when the engineer arrives on site.
  1. Complete Installation. Press when the work is finished. The system now

creates the connection and assigns it a number such as CON-B-000004.

  1. Activate. The connection is created in a Pending state. Open

Connections (/Operations/Connections) and change the status to Active.

This last step is not optional. The billing run only picks up connections that are Active. A connection left Pending will never be billed.

5.3 Connection statuses

Status Meaning Billable
Pending Created but not commissioned No
Active Live and in service Yes
Temporarily Inactive Suspended, usually for non-payment No
Permanently Inactive Closed or decommissioned No

Every status change is written to a history trail with a reason, so the connection's full life can be reconstructed.

5.4 Overdue policy

Overdue Policy (/Operations/Overdue) lists connections with unpaid bills past their due date, and lets you suspend or disconnect them.

Restoring a suspended line is done the same way — change the status back to Active. Billing resumes from the next cycle.

6. Accounts Panel

Accounts runs the money side: the monthly billing cycle, bill issuance, payments and one-off charges.

Sign in: F0000000000001 / Nexus@2026

6.1 The monthly billing cycle

This is the core routine. It runs once a month.

 Run Billing  →  Draft bills created  →  Review  →  Issue  →  Customer can pay

Step 1 — Run Billing. Open the dashboard (/Accounts/Dashboard) and press Run Billing, choosing the period (for example November 2026).

The system goes through every active connection and produces one bill per connection for that period. A connection that already has a bill for the window is skipped, so running it twice is safe.

Step 2 — Review the drafts. Open Bills in the sidebar. New bills appear with the status Draft. Nothing is visible to the customer yet.

Step 3 — Issue. Open a draft bill and press Issue bill next to the status badge. The status becomes Issued and the bill appears on the customer's dashboard.

A customer can neither see nor pay a Draft bill. This is intentional — draft is an internal document while the figures are being checked.

6.2 What goes on a bill

Line Where it comes from
Rental The plan's rate for the billing cycle
Advance paid at connection A credit, applied only as far as this period's charges allow
Usage / call charges Metered usage above the plan's included allowance
One-off charges Installation, equipment, replacement — recorded earlier from One-off Charges
Bulk discount Only for corporate accounts, and only on the advance and security deposit
Service tax 12.24% of the taxable amount

6.3 The advance, explained

When a customer orders a connection they pay an amount up front. That money is not handed back; it is credited against bills, a piece at a time.

The rule is simple: the advance is applied only as far as the current period's own charges allow.

Example — an advance of $500 and a monthly rental of $315:

Month Charges Advance applied Customer pays
Month 1 $315 rental $315 $0
Month 2 $315 rental $185 (remainder) $130 + tax

Two consequences follow, and both are correct:

  • A bill is never negative. The system will not turn a bill into a credit

note. Whatever is left of the advance waits on the order and comes off the next bill.

  • The advance is credited once. It is applied on the first bill the

connection ever raises. Later bills never carry it again.

6.4 Payments

Payments are recorded from the bill itself: open a bill, scroll to Record a Payment, enter the amount and method, and submit. The receipt number is generated automatically.

You can take a partial payment; the bill becomes Partially Paid and remains open for the balance.

6.5 One-off Charges

/Accounts/Charges records ad-hoc charges — a replacement router, an installation fee, a repair. Each charge waits as pending until the next billing run, when it is pulled onto that period's bill.

This keeps the bill the single source of truth: the customer pays once, for everything, on the invoice.

6.6 Call Metering

/Accounts/Calls records metered usage for dial-up and telephone lines — local, STD and mobile messaging minutes. These become charge lines on the next bill.

6.7 Overdue and reports

  • Overdue (/Bills?status=Overdue) — bills past their due date.
  • Reports (/Reports) — revenue by city and by year, connection growth,

customer lists.

6.8 Suppressing a bill

If a bill was raised in error, open it and use the Cancel action. A cancelled bill is excluded from all totals and no payment can be recorded against it.

7.2 Plans

/Admin/Plans manages the product catalogue.

Each plan has:

  • a name and description,
  • a connection type — Dial-Up, Broadband or Telephone,
  • an included allowance — hours for dial-up, bandwidth for broadband,
  • a security deposit,
  • prices per billing cycle (hourly, monthly, half-yearly, yearly),
  • for telephone plans, a call tariff for local, STD and mobile rates.

The row actions are icons: Edit, Retire, Delete. A retired plan disappears from new orders but existing connections on it keep working.

Tip for evaluating the system: change a plan's monthly price, then run billing again for a new period. The new price appears on the bill. This shows the rate is read live from the catalogue, not hard-coded.

7.3 Plan Changes

/Admin/PlanChanges lists customer requests to move to a different plan.

A customer cannot switch plans on their own — the change may affect the security deposit or the line's capacity, so it needs approval. Approve or reject each request from this screen.

On approval:

  • the connection's plan is changed,
  • a note is written to the connection's status history,
  • the next bill is raised on the new plan.

7.4 Outlets & Staff

/Admin/Organisation manages physical locations and the people who work at them.

  • Outlets are created with a name and city. Orders placed at the counter are

tagged with the outlet, which is what makes the Till Date Register work.

  • Employees can be linked to a login, so the system knows who did what.

7.5 Staff Logins

/Admin/Organisation/Logins creates sign-ins for staff.

You can create Accounts, Technical and Retail logins here. You cannot create an Administrator login from this screen — that would let any admin create further admins, which is a privilege-escalation risk. The seed admin is the only administrator.

The account ID is generated automatically, following the role-letter pattern in section 2.3.

8.2 Reading a bill

Open Bills & Payments and click an invoice. The invoice shows:

  • the billing period,
  • each charge line with description, quantity, rate and amount,
  • the subtotal, any bulk discount, the taxable amount and the service tax,
  • the total, what has been paid and what is still outstanding.

Below the invoice is the Payment History table — every payment with its receipt number, date, method and amount.

Draft bills are not shown to customers. An invoice appears only once the accounts department has issued it. If a customer says their bill is missing, it is usually still a draft.

8.3 How a customer pays

Payments are taken at any retail outlet or at the accounts office. The customer quotes the invoice number (or shows the invoice on their phone) and the counter staff records the payment. The receipt appears in Payment History immediately.

The invoice page also explains this, so a customer is never left wondering where the payment button is.

8.4 Requesting a plan change

  1. Open My Connection and click Upgrade plan, or go to /Customer/Upgrade.
  2. Choose from the plans available for the same connection type.
  3. The current rate and the new rate are shown side by side, so the difference

is clear before submitting.

  1. Submit the request.

The request waits for admin approval (see section 7.3). The customer can only have one open request per connection at a time.

8.5 Tracking an order

Track Order shows the order's progress. If the customer has only one order, the page opens directly on it — no need to search.

Once the order has produced a connection, the tracking page also shows the connection number and its status.

10. Understanding Your Bills

10.1 Billing cycles

A plan can be priced hourly, monthly, half-yearly or yearly. A monthly plan is billed once a month; a yearly plan is billed once a year at the yearly rate (which is its own figure, not twelve times the monthly price).

10.2 Proration

If a connection starts or ends part-way through a month, the rental is prorated for the days actually served.

10.3 Service tax

A 12.24% service tax is added to the taxable amount on every bill.

Tax is never negative. A period whose credits outweigh its charges produces a zero bill, not a tax refund.

10.4 Bulk discount

Corporate customers with many connections get a bulk rate. The tiers start at ten connections. The discount applies only to the advance and the security deposit — the recurring rental is never discounted.

Because the first tier starts at ten connections, a single-connection customer never sees a discount line on their invoice. This is correct.

10.5 When a bill shows zero

A bill can total $0 for two entirely legitimate reasons:

  1. The advance covered it. The rental was absorbed by money already paid.
  2. The period produced no charges. For example, a connection that was not

active during that window.

In both cases the invoice explains itself rather than showing an empty page.

11. Troubleshooting

"I signed in but I cannot see the payment form"

The payment form appears only when all of the following are true:

Condition How to check
The bill is not a draft The status badge says Issued, Partially Paid or Overdue
There is something left to pay The outstanding balance is greater than zero
You are not signed in as the customer Customers can view bills but never record payments

If any of these is not met, the invoice shows a panel explaining why — look at the bottom of the invoice page.

"A customer says their bill is missing"

The bill is probably still a draft. Issue it from Bills and it appears on the customer's dashboard immediately.

"A new connection is not being billed"

The connection is still Pending. Open Connections and change its status to Active. Only active connections are picked up by the billing run.

"I pressed Run Billing twice — did it duplicate?"

No. The system checks for an existing bill covering the period before creating one, and skips connections that already have one. The run reports which connections were skipped and why.

"Sign in failed with an error"

  • Check the Account ID letter matches your role (section 2.3).
  • After five failed attempts the account is temporarily locked.
  • If you see "Too many attempts", the sign-in limiter has been triggered.

Wait a minute and try again.

"I see a 403 Access Denied page"

You are signed in with a role that does not own that screen. Sign out and use the correct account — for example, the accounts ledger is not open to retail staff.

"A page shows nothing where I expected data"

Empty screens mean no records match, not an error. For example, a new customer's bill list is empty until their first bill is issued. Every empty screen in NEXUS explains what will make records appear.

Appendix B — Glossary

Term Meaning
Advance Money paid up front at order time, credited against future bills
Security deposit A refundable amount held against the equipment and line
Billing cycle How often a plan is charged — hourly, monthly, half-yearly or yearly
Billing run The monthly process that produces a bill for every active connection
Draft bill A bill produced by a billing run but not yet issued; invisible to the customer
Issued bill A bill released to the customer, against which payment can be taken
Receipt The numbered record of a payment, e.g. RCP-202610-00001
Feasibility check The site survey that decides whether a connection can be installed
Connection The physical service line, with its own number, e.g. CON-B-000004
One-off charge A non-recurring charge, such as a replacement router
Proration Charging a part-month by the day
Quoted amount The total presented to a customer at order time
